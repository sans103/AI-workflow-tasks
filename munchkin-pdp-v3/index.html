The formula aisle, decoded

What’s actually in most formula? You deserve to know.

We’re not here to scare you — you’re doing an amazing job, and any FDA-regulated formula will feed your baby. But “meets the minimum” and “the best you can give” are two different shelves. Here’s what the labels don’t lead with — in plain English.

Corn syrup, dressed up

Breastmilk’s natural sugar is lactose. In many formulas, it’s swapped for “corn syrup solids” or “glucose syrup” — cheaper to make, easier on the factory, not what your baby’s tummy was expecting. If a sweetener needs an alias, ask why.

Testing you’ll never see

Most brands say they test for the scary stuff. Very few show you anything — no results, no numbers, no receipts. “Trust us” isn’t a safety standard. You’d never accept it from a car seat.

The recalls parents remember

In 2022, contamination at a single major formula plant triggered a nationwide recall — and empty shelves coast to coast. The category earned your skepticism. A brand has to earn it back.

Marketing that outruns the science

“Inspired by breastmilk.” “Pediatrician-developed.” Lovely words — that don’t require testing on a single actual baby. Ask any formula brand one question: where’s the study? Then watch what happens.

Gentler formula starts with gentler milk. Ours comes from cows that spend their lives outdoors on New Zealand grass — which simply means cleaner, richer milk for your baby, with nothing added that shouldn’t be.

Everything you need to know

Your questions, answered honestly.

How do I switch my baby from another formula?
Gradually, over about 7 days: days 1–2 mix 25% Nurture 10 Blend with 75% of your current formula, days 3–4 go 50/50, days 5–6 go 75/25, and day 7 go all-in. Mild gassiness mid-switch is normal and passes. Full guide with video is right here on this page — and our feeding specialists will walk you through it live if you’d like company.

How do I prepare a bottle?
Wash your hands and sterilize the bottle. Boil fresh water and let it cool as directed, add water to the bottle first, then add level scoops per the ratio printed on your can, cap and shake until dissolved, and always test the temperature on your wrist before feeding. Feed within 1 hour of preparing, and never reuse a bottle baby has drunk from.

How should I store the formula?
Unopened cans: cool, dry pantry — no fridge. Opened cans: lid tight, dry scoop, use within 30 days. Prepared bottles: use within 1 hour at room temperature; unfed prepared bottles can be refrigerated and used within 24 hours. When in doubt, make a fresh one.
